From Casablanca to Islamic Economics: what should capital serve, and how can we prove it?

This portfolio comes from one practical question: when we allocate capital, what are we really serving? I started with Casablanca traffic data, GIS, SUMO, CAPEX/OPEX, ROI, and IRP. 03 — Casablanca field evidence

Casablanca Traffic ROI: where traffic, cost, and location meet.

My PFE started with a practical question: where does an advertising infrastructure investment actually make sense in Casablanca? I used SUMO, GIS, CAPEX/OPEX, TCO, ROI, and IRP to compare locations and formats, without pretending that numbers explain everything by themselves.

OOH Mode · Lower CAPEX, static exposure, accelerated break-even.

OOH / DOOH comparison

Traditional OOH emphasizes low technical complexity and minimal capital expenditure (CAPEX). It offers fast break-even logic (approx. 5.2 weeks) for mass-coverage zones where digital premiums are not yet justified.

04 — Risk-aware scoring

Risk-aware scoring, with human judgment still in the loop.

Here I test a simple question: if carbon prices, energy costs, and geoeconomic risk change, should an infrastructure ranking change too? XGBoost, DNPV, Real Options, Fuzzy-AHP/MEREC, and AROMAN help make that discussion visible. They do not decide for people.

Layer 1 · Macro-Environmental Risk

Energy scenarios, EU ETS carbon-cost exposure (€0 to €150/tCO₂), and Geopolitical Risk (GPR) indices.

Layer 2 · AI Prediction

XGBoost demand forecasting. Capability indicator: 45.8% Mean Absolute Error (MAE) reduction compared to Ridge regression baselines.

Layer 3 · Valuation & Weighting

Carbon-adjusted DNPV and Real Options Valuation combined with hybrid Fuzzy-AHP/MEREC weighting.

Layer 4 · AROMAN Ranking

Cross-validated against TOPSIS and COPRAS. AROMAN diverged from NPV-only rankings across all 25 tested projects.

Scope note: this lens does not replace financial discipline, Shariah boards, or investment committees. It only makes public-value criteria easier to see.

05 — Islamic Economics Agenda

A research path for Islamic Economics and smart infrastructure sukuk.

This is where the portfolio turns toward Islamic Economics: completed Casablanca evidence, AI-MCDM investment scoring, and a proposed SC-SIIRS framework for reviewing smart infrastructure before sukuk structuring.

G

Gap

Islamic infrastructure finance often keeps financial analysis, ESG review, technical appraisal, and Shariah governance in separate files. That makes projects harder to compare before sukuk structuring.

I

Idea

Use SC-SIIRS as a pre-screening structure that brings Maqasid al-Shariah, ESG contribution, financial viability, smart infrastructure performance, and governance risk into one place.

F

Feasibility

The proposal uses AHP expert weighting, TOPSIS ranking, MEREC/COPRAS robustness checks, and expert interviews. It does not replace Shariah boards.

T

Timeline

A 12-month academic plan: design criteria, collect expert weights, rank scenarios, test robustness, validate governance fit, and write cautious guidance.

SC-SIIRS Gantt · Islamic Finance Research Trajectory

M1–M2 · Criteria ArchitectureDefine Maqasid, ESG, financial, technical, Shariah-governance, and risk indicators.
M3–M4 · Expert WeightingAHP pairwise comparison with consistency screening and documented aggregation rules.
M5–M6 · Scenario RankingRank candidate smart infrastructure sukuk scenarios through TOPSIS.
M7–M8 · Robustness ChecksUse MEREC and COPRAS to test ranking stability and sensitivity.
M9–M10 · Governance ValidationConduct expert interviews on Shariah-governance acceptability and institutional usability.
M11–M12 · Final OutputDeliver the readiness scorecard, evidence checklist, limitations statement, and final proposal.
Scope Boundary (Al-Amānah). SC-SIIRS is a pre-screening review framework. It does not price sukuk, issue a fatwa, draft legal documents, certify Shariah compliance, or replace an investment committee.
